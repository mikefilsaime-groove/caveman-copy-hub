# Caveman Product Showcase

Turn one product into a five-card investor-style pitch and proof sequence.

This is a low-freedom, exact-format orchestrator:

```text
Caveman Product Showcase
            ↓
      Caveman Copy
        ↙       ↘
Wordsmithing   Product Visual Proof
                    ↓
       One verified visual per card
```

## Required skills

1. Read `/Users/mikefilsaime/.claude/skills/caveman-copy/SKILL.md` completely.
2. Read `/Users/mikefilsaime/.claude/skills/product-visual-proof/SKILL.md` completely.
3. Follow Caveman Copy's product-truth, value-bridge, accuracy, and scope standards.
4. Apply Product Visual Proof separately to every card's supporting visual.
5. Let Caveman Copy invoke and apply the Wordsmithing Skill before final delivery.

Do not bypass Caveman Copy, Wordsmithing, or Product Visual Proof.

## Exact five-card structure

### Card 1: instant product elevator pitch

Answer:

> “What is this product?”

Communicate the product name, recognizable category, core job, and central customer outcome in seconds. Make a stranger say, “Oh—I understand what this does.”

Do not turn card one into a feature list or vague brand statement.

#### Brand name is not the headline

On the opening card, present the product name as a distinct, prominent brand line. Then write a separate headline that explains what the product does.

- **Brand line:** `ClickCampaigns`, `BlogBaser`, `PageBuilder`, or the actual product name.
- **Headline:** the elevator pitch that answers what the product does and why the buyer should care.

Never count the product name as the headline. Never replace the product name with clever copy. A visitor must see both the brand and its meaning immediately.

### Cards 2–5: four killer features

Select the four features most likely to make the right buyer care. Rank by commercial importance, not implementation complexity, documentation order, or what is easiest to show.

Give each feature one card. Each card must establish:

- the named feature;
- its mechanism or advantage;
- the practical customer benefit;
- the promised land;
- the proof or product state that makes the claim believable.

Do not repeat the overview, split one idea across weak cards, or promote a minor feature simply to fill the sequence.

## Card construction

Use this hierarchy:

- **Brand line on card one:** the product name, large and unmistakable.
- **Feature name on cards two through five:** the named killer feature, large and unmistakable.
- **Eyebrow:** a compact category or context label; never a substitute for the brand or feature name.
- **Headline:** the clearest elevator pitch for this card’s one idea, separate from the brand or feature name.
- **Support:** feature plus mechanism in plain English.
- **Payoff:** benefit and promised land.
- **Visual proof:** a dominant real output, product state, simplified product component, mechanism scene, or bold proof treatment that makes the claim concrete.

The entire card carries the FAB-to-promised-land argument. Every individual sentence does not need all four parts.

## Per-card visual proof

Treat the supporting half of every card—right or left in a zigzag—as sales evidence, not decoration or palette balance.

Run Product Visual Proof separately for each card. Give it:

- the card's one verified claim;
- the feature mechanism, benefit, and promised land;
- the nearby final copy;
- the card's actual dimensions and responsive constraints;
- the product and design-system preservation rules.

Require a distinct visual answer for each distinct claim. The visual must show what the product or feature produces, changes, or makes possible. It may not repeat the headline in a generic panel or reuse the same dashboard skeleton across the sequence.

Do not approve the card until Product Visual Proof passes its investigation, fidelity, responsive, and showstopper gates.

## Sequence quality

Make the five cards answer a natural conversation:

1. What does it do?
2. How does it do that?
3. What makes it different?
4. Why should I care?
5. What becomes possible for me?

The feature order may vary. The selling logic may not.

## Responsive implementation

When implementing the showcase:

- keep every card readable before navigation advances;
- preserve one complete card after product or category changes;
- simplify purpose-built mockups for small screens without changing their meaning;
- avoid clipped copy, distorted screenshots, empty dead zones, and decorative UI chrome;
- verify desktop and common mobile widths.

## Final quality gate

Do not deliver until every answer is “yes”:

- Are there exactly five cards?
- Does card one explain the product in seconds?
- Does card one show the product name as a prominent brand line with a separate explanatory headline?
- Do cards two through five cover four distinct killer features?
- Does every feature reach its promised land?
- Did Product Visual Proof complete its workflow separately for every card?
- Does each visual explain what the feature produces, changes, or makes possible?
- Does each visual amplify the copy instead of repeating it?
- Is the critical visual message readable at the card's actual size?
- Is whitespace focusing attention rather than creating dead zones?
- Is the supporting side the strongest use of half the card—not a generic proof panel?
- Does every card pass Product Visual Proof's showstopper test?
- Are the five cards distinct but narratively connected?
- Are all claims verified?
- Has Caveman Copy completed the Wordsmithing pass?
