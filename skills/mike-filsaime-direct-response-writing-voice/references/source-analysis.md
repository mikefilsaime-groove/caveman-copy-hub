# Source Analysis and Confidence Notes

## Corpus

This skill was derived from three archived sales letters identified by Mike Filsaime as letters he personally took the time to write:

1. *Butterfly Marketing* — Wayback capture dated May 5, 2006  
   `https://web.archive.org/web/20060505034041/http://www.butterflymarketing.com/`
2. *The Butterfly Marketing Manuscript* — Wayback capture dated December 14, 2006  
   `https://web.archive.org/web/20061214234020/http://www.thebutterflymarketingmanuscript.com/`
3. *Carbon Copy Marketing* — Wayback capture dated February 12, 2005  
   `https://web.archive.org/web/20050212024231/http://www.carbon-copy-marketing.com/`

The analysis covers the authored headlines, lead, story, proof framing, product explanation, mechanism, offer, objection handling, guarantee, close, and postscripts.

## Explicit Exclusions

The following were not treated as Mike's writing voice:

- Testimonials
- Customer success notes
- Expert endorsements
- Quoted emails written by other people
- Third-party definitions and quotations
- Legal and earnings-disclaimer boilerplate
- Interface debris introduced by the archive or HTML conversion

Testimonials remain structurally relevant because Mike chose where and how to deploy proof, but their wording is not evidence of his diction, syntax, tone, or voice.

## Letter-Level Observations

### Carbon Copy Marketing

This letter builds trust through an ordinary-person origin: a full-time auto-dealership manager earning extra money online. It attacks implausible income hype, narrows the promise to something presented as realistic, displays concrete account proof, explains the advantages of digital products, segments benefits by experience level, stacks tools and bonuses, offers a strong guarantee, and closes through future pacing and personal responsibility.

Most distinctive signals:

- "Normal person like you" identification
- Anti-hype positioning before making a strong promise
- Exact numbers and proof artifacts
- Simple duplication as the mechanism
- Beginner-to-advanced audience segmentation
- Lifestyle translation of business benefits
- Strong guarantee and direct PS challenge

### Butterfly Marketing

This letter begins with a rebellion against daily marketing hype and turns that frustration into the "ME Marketing" distinction: own products, sites, and lists instead of remaining only a buyer or affiliate. It uses Mike's departure from the car business as an origin story, layers extensive personal proof, names and explains the Butterfly Effect, contrasts custom programming with Plug-N-Play implementation, presents a large physical and software offer, explains scarcity, and closes through identity, urgency, and risk reversal.

Most distinctive signals:

- Industry call-out and shared frustration
- Candid, sometimes abrasive truth-teller tone
- Identity mechanism: buyer versus "ME Marketer"
- Named portfolio and quantified proof ladder
- Analogy used as the central mechanism
- Old-way/new-way operational contrast
- Transparent qualification and detailed guarantee conditions
- Repeated closes with distinct emotional jobs

### The Butterfly Marketing Manuscript

This letter opens with dramatic results from the larger Butterfly Marketing launch, then creates an affordability gap: many people wanted the full system but could not pay the price. It argues for the standalone manuscript, challenges passive buyers, uses a time-travel and insanity frame to expose the cost of inaction, organizes objections into four reasons, explains the low introductory price and the seller's motives, reverses risk, adds implementation bonuses, and closes through price urgency and repeated postscripts.

Most distinctive signals:

- Results-first lead followed by explanation
- Strong acknowledgment of affordability
- Self-comparison: "What makes you different from me?"
- Identity and inaction challenge
- Numbered objection architecture
- Unusually candid explanation of pricing strategy
- Offer logic based on access to a previously expensive mechanism
- Direct, layered close

## Cross-Letter Findings

### High Confidence: Present Across the Corpus

- First-person conversation with one reader
- Identification before or alongside authority
- Shared skepticism toward hype
- Exact, concrete proof and named examples
- A memorable mechanism that organizes the promise
- Frequent anticipation of the reader's next question
- Contrast between the frustrating old path and a simpler new path
- Translation of operational benefits into personal outcomes
- Willingness to qualify or disqualify buyers
- Direct handling of price, risk, and action
- Clear, repeated calls to act
- Short paragraphs, fragments, questions, and emphatic transitions

### Medium Confidence: Strong but Context-Dependent

- Industry criticism as the primary lead
- Detailed disclosure of the seller's pricing or launch motive
- Aggressive identity challenge near the close
- Multiple PS sections
- Large bonus stacks and extreme value anchoring
- Reader-attention instructions at the top of the page

Use these when the market, offer, and format support them. They are not required markers of Mike's voice.

### Historical Surface Features, Not Core Rules

- Heavy ALL CAPS and title case
- Frequent ellipses and exclamation points
- Long one-page layouts
- Era-specific terms, technologies, and income framing
- Repeated words such as "secret," "insane," or "no brainer"
- Typographical and grammatical irregularities

Modern copy should preserve the underlying reasoning and emotional movement without mechanically reproducing these surface features.

## Analysis Limits

The corpus is strong evidence for Mike's long-form direct-response selling voice in 2005–2006. It does not by itself prove how Mike would write every current ad, short email, social promotion, webinar, or regulated claim.

Mike's current instructions and newer approved examples should update this profile over time. Treat every trait as evidence-weighted and contextual rather than immutable.
