# Caveman Website Copy

Build one coherent website argument across one page or many pages.

This is the website-level orchestrator:

```text
Caveman Website Copy
          ↓
    Caveman Copy
      ↙       ↘
Wordsmithing   Product Visual Proof
                when visuals are in scope
```

## Required skills

1. Read `/Users/mikefilsaime/.claude/skills/caveman-copy/SKILL.md` completely.
2. Use it for every hero, feature block, proof section, objection section, CTA, and other copy unit.
3. Let Caveman Copy invoke and apply the Wordsmithing Skill before final delivery.
4. When the task creates, selects, replaces, audits, or materially changes visuals, read `/Users/mikefilsaime/.claude/skills/product-visual-proof/SKILL.md` completely and apply it to every prominent product visual.

Do not bypass Caveman Copy or Wordsmithing. Do not bypass Product Visual Proof when visuals are in scope.

## Match the website scope

Choose the correct operating mode from the request:

- **Targeted rewrite:** change only the named page or sections.
- **Landing page:** create one complete conversion argument with one primary action.
- **Multi-page website:** assign a distinct job and primary argument to every page before writing.
- **Existing-site audit and rewrite:** preserve verified facts, useful content, navigation, URLs, and explicit preservation rules while improving the message.

Do not expand a targeted rewrite into a full-site rewrite. Do not flatten a multi-page site into one repeated landing page.

## Establish the site-level truth

Before drafting, identify:

- the audience and buying situation;
- the company, product, and offer hierarchy;
- the main transformation or outcome;
- the primary conversion action;
- verified features, mechanisms, proof, pricing, constraints, and objections;
- existing pages, page purposes, and content that must remain unchanged.

Inspect available documentation, product interfaces, code, analytics, claims ledgers, and existing copy. Never invent facts or silently remove important content.

## Build the message architecture

Define the site’s central argument, then give each page one clear job.

For a multi-page site, map:

- page name and URL;
- audience or visitor intent;
- one page-level promise;
- one primary action;
- supporting arguments and proof;
- the next logical page or decision.

Prevent message duplication. The homepage introduces and routes. Product pages sell specific products. Feature pages explain mechanisms. Comparison, pricing, proof, company, and legal pages perform their own jobs.

## Build each page

Use only the sections the page needs. Possible roles include:

- hero and elevator pitch;
- problem and stakes;
- solution and mechanism;
- feature blocks;
- outcomes and promised land;
- proof and demonstrations;
- how it works;
- use cases;
- comparisons;
- objection handling;
- pricing or offer presentation;
- final CTA.

Do not force a universal template. Make every section advance one argument.

### Heroes

Use Caveman Copy in hero mode. Answer what this is, what it does, who it helps when necessary, and why it matters. Preserve message scent from the visitor’s likely entry point.

### Feature sections

Use Caveman Copy in feature-block mode. Carry every feature through advantage, benefit, and promised land. Never publish a capability dump without the value bridge.

### Proof, objections, and CTAs

Use proof that supports the nearby claim. Answer objections with confidence and specificity. Make CTAs state the next action and what the visitor receives.

## Maintain continuity across pages

- Keep product names, categories, claims, offers, and terminology consistent.
- Progress the argument instead of restarting it on every page.
- Use internal links and CTAs to move visitors to the next logical decision.
- Preserve the distinction between company, suite, product, feature, and offer.
- Keep promises consistent with actual product access and fulfillment.

## Implementation and visual support

When the task includes design or code:

- preserve explicit navigation, footer, component, page, and branding rules;
- pair each major claim with the most useful product UI, result, proof, or purpose-built explanation;
- keep copy readable and complete at desktop and mobile sizes;
- avoid decorative sections that do not advance the buying argument;
- verify important responsive layouts and interactions.

### Conditional visual-proof standard

Apply Product Visual Proof whenever the work creates, selects, replaces, audits, or materially changes a visual component. Do not force visuals into copy-only work or sections where words are the clearest proof.

For each prominent visual, give Product Visual Proof:

- the nearby verified claim and final copy;
- the section's job in the page-level argument;
- the destination dimensions and responsive constraints;
- preservation rules and the approved design system;
- any known product documentation, repository, route, or access context.

Require the visual to prove and amplify its nearby claim without changing the website's message architecture. Do not approve it until Product Visual Proof passes its investigation, fidelity, responsive, and showstopper gates.

## Final quality gate

Do not deliver until every relevant answer is “yes”:

- Does the website have one coherent central argument?
- Does every page have a distinct job, promise, and primary action?
- Does every hero pass the five-second comprehension test?
- Does every feature reach a benefit and promised land?
- Are proof and objections placed near the claims they support?
- When visuals were in scope, did Product Visual Proof complete its workflow for every prominent visual?
- Does every prominent visual prove and amplify its nearby claim without changing the website's argument?
- Is repetition intentional rather than accidental?
- Are terminology, offers, and claims consistent across pages?
- Did the work remain within the requested scope and preservation rules?
- Has Caveman Copy completed the Wordsmithing pass for every final section?
